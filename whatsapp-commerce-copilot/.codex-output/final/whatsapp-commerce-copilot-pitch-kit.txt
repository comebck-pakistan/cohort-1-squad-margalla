WHATSAPP COMMERCE COPILOT — PITCH KIT
Prepared from repository evidence on 20 August 2026

1. PRODUCT ASSESSMENT BASED ON REPOSITORY

WhatsApp Commerce Copilot is a technically validated MVP for small and medium-sized WhatsApp-first merchants. The working core combines a React/Vite dashboard, FastAPI commerce backend, Node/Express gateway, PostgreSQL/Redis, store-scoped catalog retrieval, multilingual conversation state, order creation, and human takeover.

Verified strengths:
- Grounded product, variant, price, stock and policy responses.
- English, Urdu and Roman Urdu text flows.
- Conversation memory and multi-step order state.
- Catalog, conversation and order dashboard views.
- Human takeover and AI re-enable controls.
- QR and phone-number pairing interfaces.
- Multi-store data scoping.
- Image and voice gateway paths with automated tests.
- Current local verification: 294 backend tests and 85 gateway tests passing.

Important boundaries:
- No evidence of real users, revenue, growth, partnerships or commercial validation.
- No production authentication; dashboard uses store identifiers.
- Live QR/device lifecycle still requires real-device validation and Evolution API/Redis/PostgreSQL availability.
- Image and voice paths depend on configured Gemini access and live transport behavior.
- Evolution API/Baileys is unofficial and carries policy, ban and breakage risk.
- Production security, onboarding, analytics and operational reliability need hardening.

Positioning:
An AI sales assistant that turns a merchant’s existing WhatsApp into an always-available, multilingual commerce channel.

6. Q&A PREPARATION

Q: Is this just another chatbot?
A: No. The differentiator is the operational loop: store-scoped catalog and policy retrieval, variant and inventory checks, conversation memory, structured order state, merchant dashboard and human takeover. The AI is constrained by authoritative merchant data.

Q: What traction do you have?
A: We do not yet claim users, revenue or market validation. We have a working MVP and technical validation through end-to-end workflows and automated tests. The next proof point is a small set of measured merchant pilots.

Q: How do you prevent hallucinated prices or availability?
A: The model does not query the database freely. The deterministic layer retrieves a closed candidate set from the store-scoped catalog. Returned product and variant identifiers are validated, and authoritative database price and stock values win. If the system is uncertain, it clarifies or hands off.

Q: Why will merchants pay?
A: That is a hypothesis to test. The value proposition is recovered owner time, faster response and fewer order-recording errors. Pilots should measure those outcomes and willingness to pay before final pricing.

Q: Why not use WhatsApp Business API directly?
A: The current MVP uses a transport-isolated Evolution/Baileys gateway for fast technical validation. The backend contract is deliberately separated so the transport can migrate to Meta’s official API. Production deployment should prioritize that migration where appropriate.

Q: What happens if the AI fails?
A: The rule-first deterministic path continues without a model key for common catalog and order flows. Low confidence and unavailable information trigger clarification, honest fallback or human handoff.

Q: Can stores see each other’s data?
A: Repository data access is scoped by store_id and cross-store behavior is tested. However, production authentication and authorization still need hardening before commercial deployment.

Q: Does it support voice and images today?
A: Gateway implementations and automated tests exist for transcription and vision. They depend on Gemini configuration, media download and live transport behavior, so we label them configuration-dependent until real-device pilots establish reliability.

Q: What is the moat?
A: Near term, it is the integrated commerce workflow and grounding discipline. Over time, the stronger moat would come from merchant onboarding, clean catalog/variant data, workflow integrations, reliability, and a consented, anonymized dataset of real multilingual commerce conversations. That dataset does not exist yet.

Q: How big is the market?
A: We have not inserted an unsupported market statistic. We propose a bottom-up model: reachable WhatsApp-first merchants multiplied by a pilot-validated subscription. Both inputs require current research and field validation.

Q: What will pilots measure?
A: Response time, automation rate, human handoff rate, catalog-match failures, order completion, order correction/error rate, media-processing cost and latency, merchant time saved, customer drop-off, retention and willingness to pay.

DIFFICULT OBJECTIONS

Objection: Unofficial WhatsApp automation makes the product uninvestable.
Response: It is a real risk, not a footnote. The current transport is for MVP validation. The gateway is isolated from business logic specifically to support migration to Meta’s official WhatsApp Business/Cloud API. A pilot plan should include policy review and a transport migration milestone.

Objection: The merchant’s catalog will be incomplete or wrong.
Response: Grounding can only be as good as the source data. Merchant onboarding must include catalog completeness, variant/stock checks and clear freshness ownership. When data is missing, the Copilot should say so and hand off rather than guess.

Objection: AI cost and latency will destroy margins.
Response: The architecture is rule-first. Common catalog and order actions do not require free-form generation. Gemini is optional and constrained to interpretation/phrasing or media. Plans need message/media limits, observability and usage-based pricing.

Objection: Roman Urdu is too variable for heuristics.
Response: Current language detection is heuristic and tested against representative inputs, but real-message performance is unproven. Pilots should collect consented, anonymized examples, measure failure categories and improve the classifier without claiming an accuracy number prematurely.

Objection: Merchants already answer quickly themselves.
Response: Some do, and those are not the first target. The beachhead is merchants with repetitive questions, missed off-hour inquiries, informal catalogs and insufficient staff. Customer discovery should qualify this pain before onboarding.
